About Me

Turning models into systems people can actually rely on.

Background

Ten years in industry, six of them in machine learning. I started as a software engineer working onsite for Nissan in Amsterdam and then Paris, where much of the work was manual calculation and hand-maintained rules. I replaced part of it with regression models that did the job faster and more consistently, and the modelling turned out to be more interesting than the plumbing.

Since then I have worked across the full lifecycle — messy data, feature engineering, models, deployment and the dashboards people actually look at. Credit risk scoring and financial crime monitoring at KBC Bank, then leading demand forecasting for Nissan, where model output fed systems that could not be taken offline. I did an MSc in Computer Science at Leiden University to deepen the theory behind work I had been doing in practice.

What interests me is the part where good engineering matters as much as a good model. A model that scores well but cannot be explained to an auditor is not finished, and neither is one that works in a notebook and falls over on live data. The projects I have enjoyed most are the ones where someone's work got measurably easier because the system was built properly, not just accurately.

Outside work I read papers, play sport, and keep a few side projects running. I learn best by building things rather than reading about them.
